Biochemical Role of Free L-Amino Acids and Proline in Crop Recovery from Heat & Drought Stress

هیئت علمی و تدوین: Crop Physiology & Abiotic Stress Research Division, Pishgaman Paydar Falat Nik | آخرین بروزرسانی: 2026-10-11

چکیده پژوهش و مبانی کاربردی

Abiotic heatwaves and drought spells represent the leading causes of 40-60% yield losses in commercial orchards and open-field vegetables. This paper evaluates the kinetic absorption of enzymatically hydrolyzed free amino acids and their osmotic regulating functions under extreme climatic stress.

1. Oxidative Stress and Stomatal Closure Dynamics

When ambient canopy temperatures exceed 35°C, crops initiate stomatal closure to minimize transpiration. Consequently, CO2 starvation arrests photosynthesis, causing excess photon accumulation that generates cytotoxic reactive oxygen species (ROS) and severe thermal chlorosis.

2. Protective Mechanisms of Proline and Glutamate

L-Proline serves as a premier compatible osmolyte, preserving cytoplasmic hydration and stabilizing Rubisco enzyme folding against thermal denaturation. L-Glutamic acid acts as the direct biochemical precursor to chlorophyll synthesis, restoring foliage vigor.

3. Specialized Formulation of Doping Biostimulant

Pishgaman Doping formulation provides biologically active L-configured free amino acids combined with chelated micronutrient cofactors. Its formulation penetrates leaf cuticle barriers rapidly, restoring vegetative vigor within 48 hours of application.

4. Agronomic Application Protocol

Foliar application at 1.5 - 2 Liters per 1,000 Liters of water 24-48 hours before predicted heat spikes, or immediately following acute stress, ensures optimal crop recovery and fruit retention.
